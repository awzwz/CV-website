# J-1 internship pathway and funded programs for a Kazakhstan master's/PhD student (US internship, Spring/Summer 2027)

> Research date: 2026-10-27. Method note: direct page fetching was blocked by the sandbox network proxy (DNS/403 for federalregister.gov, govinfo.gov, culturalvistas.org, etc.), so all findings below come from web-search result snippets/summaries of the cited pages. The per-turn web search budget was exhausted before every gap could be closed, so the Gaps sections are marked explicitly. Primary sources (State Dept, eCFR, kz.usembassy.gov, bolashak.gov.kz) are cited where search surfaced them. Treat all fees as "last published", not guaranteed for 2027.

## 1. How the J-1 Intern / Trainee / Student Intern pathway works (eligibility, duration, DS-7002, English, 212(e) for Kazakhstan)

### Takeaway
A Kazakhstan citizen currently enrolled in a master's or PhD program in Kazakhstan fits the J-1 **Intern** category (enrolled full-time abroad, or graduated no more than 12 months before the start date). Interns can stay up to 12 months, and the program runs on a DS-7002 training plan issued through a State Dept-designated sponsor. **Kazakhstan was removed from the Exchange Visitor Skills List on Dec 9, 2024, and the removal is retroactive.** A self-funded or employer-paid J-1 intern from Kazakhstan therefore generally faces **no 212(e) two-year home-residency requirement**. The requirement can still apply on other grounds, chiefly US or Kazakh **government funding** such as Fulbright or Bolashak.

### Cited Findings
**Intern category (22 CFR 62.22)**
- To be an intern, a person must be currently enrolled full-time in a degree- or certificate-granting post-secondary institution outside the US, **or** have graduated from one no more than 12 months before the program begin date on the DS-2019. The internship must be a "structured and guided work-based internship program in his or her specific academic field". — [eCFR 22 CFR 62.22](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-62/subpart-B/section-62.22); [Cornell LII 22 CFR 62.22](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.22)
- Maximum duration: **internship 12 months, training 18 months**. Training in Agriculture and in Hospitality & Tourism is capped at 12 months. Agriculture can reach 18 months if at least 6 months are classroom-based. — [Cornell LII 22 CFR 62.22](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.22); [eCFR 62.22](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-62/subpart-B/section-62.22)
- **DS-7002 (Training/Internship Placement Plan, T/IPP):** the sponsor must fully complete Form DS-7002 and obtain all required signatures **before** issuing the DS-2019. The trainee or intern must present the signed form at the visa interview if asked. An intern's T/IPP must identify the specific tasks and activities. — [eCFR 62.22](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-62/subpart-B/section-62.22)
- **English:** sponsors must verify English "sufficient to function on a day-to-day basis" in the training environment. Accepted evidence is a recognized English test, signed documentation from an academic institution or English school, or a documented interview held in person or by videoconference (telephone only if video isn't viable). — [eCFR 62.22](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-G/part-62/subpart-B/section-62.22)
- Cultural Vistas describes interns the same way: currently enrolled at a foreign college or university, or graduated within one year before the program starts; internships last up to 12 months. — [Cultural Vistas – J-1 application process](https://culturalvistas.org/impact-learning/news-stories/j1-visa-application-process)

**Trainee category**
- A trainee needs either a degree plus at least 1 year of related work experience outside the US, or 5 years of related experience outside the US without a degree. Minimum age is 18. Training lasts up to 18 months, and per CIEE the visa adds about a month before and after. — [CIEE Professional Career Training USA](https://www.ciee.org/in-the-usa/research-training/intern-professional-training/our-programs/ciee-professional-career-training-usa); the same definitions appear in AIESEC US program materials ([AIESEC US host info doc](https://aiesecugabd.yolasite.com/resources/10100675_AIESEC_US_J-1_Program_Host_Company_Information.doc))

**Student Intern category (College & University Student, 22 CFR 62.23(i)), sponsored by US universities**
- The participant must be enrolled in a degree program at an accredited post-secondary institution outside the US, and the US internship must serve the educational objectives of that home degree. The field can differ from the major as long as it serves the degree. — [Kennesaw State J-1 Student Intern](https://campus.kennesaw.edu/current-students/academics/global-education/international-student-scholar-services/j-1/student-interns.php); [SIU host form](https://cie.siu.edu/_common/documents/forms/cie-61.pdf); [Cornell LII 22 CFR 62.23](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/62.23)
- Rules: up to **12 months per degree level, no extensions**. Generally full-time, at least 32 hours/week, with clerical work capped at 20%. The student must return home and finish the degree. Good academic standing and approval from the home dean or advisor are required. The sponsor must be a US institution designated in the College & University Student category, and it must secure the placement before issuing the DS-2019. — [SIU host form](https://cie.siu.edu/_common/documents/forms/cie-61.pdf); [St. Cloud State application](https://www.stcloudstate.edu/internationalstudents/_files/documents/j-1-student-intern-application-new.pdf); [Harvard HIO enrollment certificate](https://www.hio.harvard.edu/sites/default/files/J-1%20Student%20Intern%20Enrollment%20and%20Degree%20Certificate.pdf); [Temple ISSS](https://global.temple.edu/isss/faculty-staff-and-researchers/j-1-research-scholars-professor/j-1-student-interns)
- Universities may charge their own admin fee. Example: the University of Minnesota lists a **$560** administrative fee for J-1 student interns (page dated Sept 2025). — [UMN ISSS Exchange Visitor Fees](https://isss.umn.edu/exchange-visitor-fees)

**212(e) two-year home residency and Kazakhstan**
- The State Dept revised the Exchange Visitor Skills List effective **Dec 9, 2024**, superseding the 2009 list. Countries were removed and none added; most sources say 37 were removed, one says 34. — [Federal Register notice 2024-28718](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2024/12/09/2024-28718/public-notice-of-revised-exchange-visitor-skills-list); [travel.state.gov news](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/exchange-visitor-skills-list-update-in-federal-register.html); [Foster Global](https://www.fosterglobal.com/blog/department-of-state-issues-revised-j-1-exchange-visitor-skills-list-removing-home-residence-requirement-for-34-countries/)
- **Kazakhstan is among the countries removed.** It appears in the published removed-country lists (Asia: Bahrain, Bangladesh, China, India, Indonesia, **Kazakhstan**, Laos, Malaysia, ...). — [University of Alabama Intl. office](https://international.ua.edu/?p=26074); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/j-1-exchange-visitors-30-countries-no-longer-subject-two-year-foreign-home); [Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/united-states-dos-issues-revised-j-1-exchange-visitor-skills-list-with-retroactive-effect-china-and-india-among-other-countries-have-been-removed-from-the-list.html); [MIT ISCHO](https://ischo.mit.edu/news/j-1-exchange-visitor-skills-list-updated-certain-countries-removed-retroactively)
- The removal is **retroactive** for nationals of removed countries. It does not help when only a skill area was removed and the country stayed on the list. — [CU Boulder ISSS](https://www.colorado.edu/isss/2024/12/12/updated-exchange-visitor-skills-list-released); [Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/united-states-dos-issues-revised-j-1-exchange-visitor-skills-list-with-retroactive-effect-china-and-india-among-other-countries-have-been-removed-from-the-list.html)
- 212(e) **still applies on other grounds**, such as receiving funding from the US government or the home government, or graduate medical training. — [CU Boulder ISSS](https://www.colorado.edu/isss/2024/12/12/updated-exchange-visitor-skills-list-released); [WR Immigration FAQ](https://wolfsdorf.com/department-of-state-revised-exchange-visitor-skills-list-update-frequently-asked-questions/)
- Neighbouring Kyrgyzstan, Uzbekistan and Tajikistan do **not** appear on the removed lists. No source states outright that they remain on the list. — [search synthesis of law-firm lists, e.g. Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/work-visas/1556200/dos-issues-revised-j-1-exchange-visitor-skills-list-with-retroactive-effect-china-and-india-among-other-countries-have-been-removed-from-the-list)

### Inferences
- For a Kazakhstan master's or PhD student: if the US company or self-funding pays, the J-1 Intern route is **free of 212(e) on Skills-List grounds**. That keeps a later H-1B, O-1 or green-card path open. A J-1 **funded by Bolashak (Kazakh government) or Fulbright (US government)** would very likely carry 212(e) on government-funding grounds. The DS-2019 annotation and the consular officer's 212(e) note on the visa should be checked in each case.
- **Intern** (via a third-party sponsor such as Cultural Vistas) is the default category for a corporate internship. **Student Intern** fits mainly when the host is a US university (e.g., a research-lab internship) whose international office issues the DS-2019 itself. **Trainee** applies only to someone who already holds a degree plus 1 year of foreign work experience, or who is more than 12 months past graduation.
- Someone finishing a master's in Kazakhstan in June 2027 can still start a J-1 internship any time up to 12 months after graduation. The "Intern" window therefore covers Summer 2027 whether or not they are still enrolled then.

### Gaps
- I could not open the official 2024 Skills List or Federal Register text (network block). The Kazakhstan removal is confirmed only through several consistent secondary sources (university ISSS pages and law firms). Verify on the State Dept "Skills List by Country" page.
- One secondary blog claimed that interns of 6+ months and all trainees "must be remunerated". I could not verify this against the regulation text, so it is not relied on.
- I did not research alternative J-1 categories for researchers (Research Scholar, Short-Term Scholar, Student Non-Degree) for PhD students doing lab research.

## 2. J-1 sponsor organizations (sponsor-only vs placement, fees, processing, Kazakhstan, big-company usage)

### Takeaway
Most large third-party J-1 sponsors are **sponsorship-only**: the student finds the host and the sponsor vets it and issues the DS-7002/DS-2019. The main ones are Cultural Vistas, InterExchange, CIEE (Intern/Professional Career Training USA), Intrax (visa-only service), ASSE and the American Immigration Council. Published sponsor program fees run roughly **$900–$1,900** (Cultural Vistas), plus insurance, the $220 SEVIS fee and the $185 visa fee. CIEE's all-inclusive maximums run up to about $4,400–6,000 depending on country. Processing is typically **2–8 weeks before the visa interview**. AIESEC US and IAESTE (run in the US by Cultural Vistas) are the main **placement-type** exchanges reachable from Kazakhstan. No source found confirms which sponsor Google, Meta or Amazon use. Microsoft is reported to hold **its own** J-1 designation.

- **Sponsor only:** CV does not match candidates with hosts, except in a few grant programs such as Korea WEST. The participant must bring their own internship offer. — [Cultural Vistas resources](https://culturalvistas.org/resources-j-1-interns-trainees/); [U of Toronto Learning Abroad – Cultural Vistas](https://learningabroad.utoronto.ca/?p=10107)

**InterExchange (Career Training USA – Intern/Trainee)**
- Sponsorship of self-found placements. The fee covers application processing (review of host, training plan and eligibility), DS-2019 and DS-7002 issuance and shipping, the **$220 SEVIS fee paid on the participant's behalf**, accident and sickness insurance (program plus a 30-day grace period), orientation, a US SIM card and 24/7 support. — [InterExchange fees](https://www.interexchange.org/travel-abroad/intern-usa/fees/); [InterExchange inclusions](https://www.interexchange.org/programs/career-training/intern-trainee/inclusions/)

- Proof of funds: at least **$1,500 per month** of the program, or $750 per month if housing is provided. — [InterExchange](https://www.interexchange.org/travel-abroad/intern-usa/fees/)
- The fee amount itself comes from an online calculator and was not captured. — (gap)

### Inferences
- If a Kazakhstani student gets an offer from a US company **that does not hold its own designation**, the realistic path is to apply directly to Cultural Vistas, InterExchange, the American Immigration Council or Intrax. Cultural Vistas is the cheapest published option (student, ≤3 months: $900 plus insurance plus $220 SEVIS). Using a Kazakh agency adds cost and, given the 2026 Work and Travel scandal, risk.
- If the host is **new to the sponsor**, add time for host onboarding and a possible site visit, and note that CV's new-host fee must be paid by the company. This favours established companies or US universities as hosts.
- Big tech typically handles J-1 sponsorship via its own designation or a contracted sponsor, so the intern does not choose a sponsor. Ask the recruiter which sponsor appears on the DS-2019.
- The prompt's "Council for International Exchange" most likely refers to CIEE (Council on International Educational Exchange). The **Alliance for International Exchange** is an advocacy umbrella, not a sponsor.

## 3. Kazakhstan-specific logistics (consulates, wait times, fees, interview waiver, 2025–2026 policy changes, travel bans)

### Takeaway
J-1 visas for Kazakh citizens are processed at the **US Embassy in Astana and the Consulate General in Almaty**. Kazakh citizens and residents get priority slots. Third-party trackers show Almaty F/M/J interview waits of **about 2 weeks** in mid/late 2026. **Kazakhstan is NOT on the June 2025 or the December 2025 (Proclamation 10998, effective Jan 1 2026) travel-ban lists.** It is also not named on the B-1/B-2 visa-bond list. It **is** on the Jan 2026 **immigrant**-visa pause list of 75 countries, but that pause does not affect J-1. Policy changes that do affect a 2027 J-1 applicant:
- mandatory in-person interview (waivers narrowed from Sept 2, 2025)
- public social-media profiles and an online-presence review for F/M/J
- applying in the country of nationality or residence
- a statutory $250 "visa integrity fee", with uneven collection
- seasonal appointment squeezes

### Cited Findings
**Where and how to apply**
- Nonimmigrant visas are handled in Astana and at the Consulate General in Almaty. Limited slots are reserved for nonresidents to prioritize Kazakh citizens and residents. — [US Embassy & Consulate in Kazakhstan – Visas](https://kz.usembassy.gov/visas/); [Consular Section](https://kz.usembassy.gov/consular-section/)
- Since May 2025, Astana and Almaty finalize appointments at 5:00 pm three business days before the interview, and the DS-160 must be complete by then. — [kz.usembassy.gov – upcoming NIV appointment](https://kz.usembassy.gov/important-information-about-your-upcoming-non-immigrant-visa-appointment/)
- The embassy visa page flags the social-media "public" requirement for F, J and M applicants and references Proclamation 10998. — [kz.usembassy.gov – Visas](https://kz.usembassy.gov/visas/)

**Wait times (no official figure captured)**
- Almaty, F/M/J interview: "< 0.5 month" per one tracker (updated about July 21, 2026) and "15 days" per another (Sept 2026), with a 2-day wait for dropbox or waiver cases. The Astana J figure is blank or unknown. — [VisaGrader – Kazakhstan](https://visagrader.com/us-visa-appointment-wait-times/kazakhstan-kaz); [AFNO Visa Guide – Kazakhstan, Sept 2026](https://www.afnovisaguide.com/us-visa-wait-times/kazakhstan)
- The official State Dept Global Visa Wait Times page was last updated 21-OCT-2026, but its Almaty/Astana figures could not be read through search. — [travel.state.gov Global Visa Wait Times](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/global-visa-wait-times.html)

**Fees**
- J-1 visa application (MRV) fee is **$185**. — [Cultural Vistas fees](https://culturalvistas.org/j-1-fees-intern-trainee-research-scholar-specialist); [UMN ISSS](https://isss.umn.edu/exchange-visitor-fees)
- SEVIS I-901 fee is **$220** for J-1 interns and trainees, payable at least 3 days before the visa application. Pages listing $180 are outdated. — [Yale OISS](https://oiss.yale.edu/printpdf/getting-started/new-students/applying-for-your-us-visa/applying-for-a-j-1-visa/paying-the-sevis-fee); [UH Manoa](https://manoa.hawaii.edu/fsis/j1/sponsor/sevisfee/); [InterExchange](https://www.interexchange.org/travel-abroad/intern-usa/fees/)
- **Visa Integrity Fee:** at least $250, created by the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025). It applies to J-1, has a statutory start of Oct 1, 2025, is indexed to CPI from FY2026, and is charged **at issuance** (not charged if refused). Collection is reported as **uneven** across consulates, and no uniform official start notice was found. — [Ellis – Visa Integrity Fee](https://www.ellis.com/resources/visa-integrity-fee); [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/250-visa-integrity-fee-what-employers-and-foreign-nationals-need-know-about-latest); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/18/visa-integrity-fee-what-to-know-about-new-travel-fee-to-enter-the-us-.html)

## 5. Realistic timeline if applying Nov 2026 – Mar 2027 for a Summer 2027 internship

### Takeaway
Plan backwards from the start date. From signed offer to arrival takes about **6–12 weeks**:
- sponsor intake and host vetting: about 2–4 weeks, longer if the host is new to the sponsor
- DS-2019 and DS-7002 issuance and shipping
- SEVIS payment at least 3 days before the interview
- Almaty/Astana interview: about 2+ weeks wait
- possible 221(g) administrative processing after the social-media review

For a **June 2027** start, a signed offer should be in hand by about **mid-March 2027 at the latest**, ideally by **February**. For a **Spring 2027** start (Feb–April), the offer is needed by **late Nov – Dec 2026**. Funded Bolashak or Fulbright routes for 2027 are essentially closed except the Bolashak scientific internship deadline of **Oct 30, 2026**.

### Cited Findings
- Sponsor plus visa takes about 6–8 weeks on average (InterExchange), with 3-day expedited review available for a fee. — [InterExchange](https://www.interexchange.org/travel-abroad/intern-usa/fees/)
- American Immigration Council standard review is about 10 business days or fewer. — [AIC intern FAQs](https://immigrationpolicy.org/intern-faqs)

### Inferences
- **Suggested back-plan for a June 1, 2027 start:**

  | When | Step |
  |---|---|
  | By about Feb 15 – Mar 15, 2027 | Signed offer |
  | Within 1 week of the offer | Sponsor application, with the host completing its part |
  | +2–4 weeks | DS-7002 signed and DS-2019 issued (shipping or e-delivery) |
  | Immediately after | SEVIS fee, DS-160, Almaty or Astana appointment |
  | About April–May | Interview, leaving a 3–6 week buffer for possible 221(g) |
  | 30 days before program start | Earliest entry |

- An offer arriving after about **early April 2027** makes a June start tight but possible with expedited sponsor review. After about **early May**, a July start is more realistic.
- **Big US tech summer-2027 internship recruiting** generally peaks in the preceding autumn, so many positions may already be filled by Nov 2026. This is based on general industry patterns, not a source found here. Nov 2026 – Mar 2027 applications are therefore better aimed at smaller and mid-size companies, startups, US university research labs (which can use the J-1 Student Intern or Intern categories), and AIESEC Global Talent or IAESTE offers.
- A **Spring 2027** start (Feb–Apr) is realistic only if an offer is signed by about early Dec 2026.
